## B. Gap Statement

Taken separately, the literatures explain important pieces but do not yet provide the proposed model.

Systems psychodynamics gives group dynamics, anxiety, defense, task orientation, and open-systems lineage. It does not give a formal or semi-formal account of ordered organizational action sequences. Behavioral theory of the firm gives bounded rationality, coalitions, search, routines, and procedural decision-making. It does not give the appetite/feasibility/constraint diagnostic triad or a psychodynamic metaphor disciplined by action composition. Paradox theory gives contradictory demands and dynamic equilibrium. It does not by itself specify how growth demand, technical-operational reality, and legitimacy constraint are translated into a sequence of commitments. Organizational learning gives acquisition, interpretation, distribution, and memory. It does not define intelligence as the quality of mediation protocols under internal force conflict.

Sensemaking explains how ambiguity becomes action-guiding interpretation. It does not distinguish the three proposed pressures or specify how their conflict is sequenced. Strategic communication explains information transmission under partially conflicting preferences. It does not include legitimacy, institutional pressure, unconscious defense, power asymmetry, technical feasibility, or organizational memory. Cybernetics gives diagnostic regulation and viability. It does not give the appetite/feasibility/constraint vocabulary, and the proposed paper should not pretend that vocabulary is a replacement for VSM.

The safest gap claim is therefore narrow: using the verified source pack, we have not yet found a single framework that combines (1) a three-force diagnostic vocabulary, (2) partial goal conflict and communication limits, (3) practical irreversibility and non-commutative sequencing of organizational actions, and (4) an organizational-intelligence claim about stable mediation protocols transforming conflict into better action. This is a gap in the synthesis we have found, not proof that no such framework exists anywhere.

The gap is also practical. Existing literatures often ask whether organizations learn, regulate, make sense, manage paradox, satisfy stakeholders, or transmit information. The proposed paper asks a more diagnostic question: when appetite, feasibility, and constraint collide, what protocol determines which force is heard, when it is heard, how it is translated, what action sequence follows, and what commitments become hard to undo?

The paper's central diagnostic object is the **mediation protocol**. A mediation protocol is the recurring communicative and procedural structure through which appetite, feasibility, and constraint are surfaced, weighted, sequenced, translated, and resolved into organizational commitments. This object differs from a routine because it specifically concerns conflict transformation; it differs from a capability because it is not simply a capacity to perform, but a structure for deciding how incompatible pressures become action; and it differs from a general decision process because it focuses on the triadic collision among growth, implementability, and legitimacy.

## C. Contribution Statement

**1. Diagnostic vocabulary: appetite, feasibility, constraint**

The paper proposes three analytic constructs. Appetite names growth, revenue, speed, market demand, competitive urgency, expansion, and dominance pressure. Feasibility names technical-operational reality: engineering capacity, infrastructure, maintainability, security implementation, work-system design, process discipline, and organizational execution. Constraint names law, compliance, legitimacy, ethics, reputation, customer trust, stakeholder pressure, institutional expectations, and social permission.

This vocabulary is not meant to replace the existing literatures. Its value is diagnostic compression. It lets the paper ask whether a decision process is dominated by growth appetite, blocked or translated by feasibility, disciplined by legitimacy constraint, or distorted because one force is structurally underpowered. The updated source pack's power note matters here: the model should include `p`, the relative power distribution among appetite, feasibility, and constraint actors. The forces are not a balanced committee by default.

The primary unit of analysis is the high-stakes decision episode: a bounded sequence of meetings, communications, commitments, reviews, escalations, and actions through which an organization converts conflicting pressures into an ordered course of action.

**2. Formal intuition: irreversible and non-commutative action**

The paper can safely introduce a formal intuition, not a finished formal theory. Organizational actions are often ordered commitments. The sequence "review, then ship" may differ from "ship, then review." The sequence "build compliance, then sell an enterprise deal" may differ from "sell the deal, then build compliance." In the model, this means action composition is often non-commutative. Some actions are also practically irreversible or non-invertible: launches, public promises, reputational events, technical debt, compliance exposure, and reorganizations may not be cleanly undone.

This is a model definition and future-work direction. Ghani et al. (2018), semigroup theory, process algebra, and Bayesian open games should stay in Ring 3 unless the paper actually formalizes states, actions, composition, protocols, and information conditions. The main paper only needs enough formal intuition to make sequencing diagnostically visible.

The first paper does not attempt to prove a formal algebra of organizations. It introduces irreversibility and non-commutativity as diagnostic properties of action sequences. Formal treatment through semigroups, process algebra, or compositional games is reserved for later work unless the present paper defines the relevant state space, transformations, equivalence relations, and information conditions.

**3. Organizational-intelligence claim: stable mediation protocols transform conflict into better action**

The proposed thesis is that organizational intelligence can be defined, provisionally, as a collective's capacity to develop mediation protocols that generate action sequences which remain adaptive across growth, implementability, and legitimacy pressures over time.

"Protocol" must be defined carefully. A routine is a recurring action pattern. A capability is a reliable capacity to perform or adapt. A protocol is a rule, process, or communicative structure for transforming conflict into action. A mediation protocol is a protocol that governs how appetite, feasibility, and constraint are surfaced, sequenced, weighted, translated, and resolved. This distinction is necessary because the routine and capability literatures already cover adjacent ground.

## E. Strongest Safe Thesis Paragraph

We propose a diagnostic framework for organizational intelligence in which organizations are understood as systems that transform partially conflicting pressures toward appetite, feasibility, and constraint into ordered commitments. The framework draws on systems psychodynamics, behavioral theory of the firm, paradox theory, organizational learning, sensemaking, strategic communication, legitimacy theory, and organizational cybernetics, but it does not claim that corporations literally have psyches, that organizational contradiction is newly discovered, or that a formal mathematical theory has already been validated. Its narrower contribution is to define the **mediation protocol** as a diagnostic object: the recurring communicative and procedural structure through which growth demands, technical-operational realities, and legitimacy constraints are surfaced, weighted, sequenced, translated, and resolved. Because organizational actions can be practically irreversible and because action order can matter, the framework treats such protocols as candidates for organizational intelligence: structures that help a collective generate action sequences that remain viable across growth, implementability, and legitimacy pressures over time.
