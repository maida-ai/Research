# Literature Search: Appetite, Feasibility, and Constraint

Tentative paper title: **"Appetite, Feasibility, and Constraint: A Psychodynamic Operator Model of Organizational Intelligence."**

Important limitation: this memo is based on verified scholarly metadata, abstracts, publisher pages, PubMed records, and stable library/catalog pages accessed during this search. I did not verify every claim against every full text. Claims below are therefore phrased conservatively. Anything depending on page-specific interpretation should be checked against the full source before submission.

## A. Search Strategy Used

I searched across publisher and scholarly-index surfaces rather than general web summaries: Academy of Management, INFORMS/PubsOnline, Sage Journals, Wiley Online Library, SpringerLink, MIT Press, Routledge, PubMed, Oxford Academic, Cambridge Core, AEA, Taylor & Francis, Google Books, Open Library, WorldCat-style records, arXiv, and Project Euclid/CiNii where relevant.

Search terms followed the six requested streams:

- Systems psychodynamics and psychoanalytic organization studies: `systems psychodynamics organizations`, `Tavistock group relations organizations`, `Bion work group basic assumptions`, `psychoanalytic organization theory`, `organizational psychodynamics`, `Manfred Kets de Vries leadership psychoanalysis organizations`, `Gareth Morgan psychic prisons organizations`.
- Collective and organizational intelligence: `collective intelligence organizations`, `organizational intelligence theory`, `distributed cognition organizations`, `group intelligence emergence`, `collective intelligence multi-agent systems`.
- Conflict, paradox, and competing demands: `organizational paradox theory conflicting demands`, `institutional logics conflicting demands organizations`, `organizational ambidexterity exploration exploitation`, `tensions in organizations management theory`.
- Game theory and communication under conflicting goals: `cheap talk game theory conflicting preferences`, `strategic communication organizations`, `information transmission conflicting incentives`, `Crawford Sobel cheap talk`, `coordination games organizations`.
- Mathematics of irreversible and non-commutative action: `semigroup theory transformations irreversible actions`, `monoids computation actions`, `category theory processes systems`, `process algebra concurrency noncommutative`, `compositional game theory open games`, `algebra of processes`.
- Organizational cybernetics and diagnostic models: `Stafford Beer viable system model organization diagnosis`, `organizational cybernetics diagnostic model`, `socio-technical systems theory organizations`, `complex adaptive systems organizations`.

Selection criteria:

- Prefer peer-reviewed journal articles and academic books from university or scholarly publishers.
- Include sources that either support the proposed model, challenge it, or mark boundaries that prevent overclaiming.
- Avoid Wikipedia, blogs, LinkedIn, and uncited webpages as evidence. Some library/publisher catalog records are used only to verify book existence, title, publisher, date, ISBN, or table of contents.
- Mark confidence lower when I verified only metadata or an abstract rather than the full text.

## D. Gaps in the Literature

- There appears to be a gap between systems psychodynamics and formal compositional/game/process models. Systems psychodynamics has rich clinical and diagnostic language, while compositional game theory and process algebra have formal tools, but I did not find a direct bridge between Tavistock-style organization psychodynamics and monoidal/category-theoretic process models.
- Existing paradox and ambidexterity research handles competing demands, but typically not through a triadic appetite/feasibility/constraint operator model.
- Collective intelligence research often focuses on small groups, crowds, platforms, or distributed cognition. The specific mechanism of transforming internal organizational conflict into intelligence remains underdeveloped as a formal diagnostic framework.
- Organizational cybernetics offers diagnostic models of viability and regulation, but it may not foreground legal/ethical/reputational constraint as a psychodynamic/institutional force in the same way this paper proposes.
- Game-theoretic communication models address incentive conflict and information transmission, but they usually abstract away unconscious defenses, identity, legitimacy, and institutional logics.
- The mathematical literature supports irreversible/non-invertible and non-commutative process modeling, but applying it to organizations will require careful construction of organizational states, actions, operators, and equivalence relations.

## F. Red Flags: Overclaim or Duplication Risks

- **Do not claim a new discovery that organizations contain unconscious conflict.** Systems psychodynamics, Tavistock group relations, Bion, Kets de Vries, and Morgan already cover psychoanalytic organization imagery and group defense.
- **Do not claim a new discovery that organizations face contradictory demands.** Paradox theory, institutional complexity, ambidexterity, and exploration/exploitation already provide mature vocabularies.
- **Do not overstate the Freudian analogy.** The paper should say "id-like," "ego-like," and "superego-like" only as a diagnostic metaphor, then shift to appetite, feasibility, and constraint as model-defined constructs.
- **Do not imply collective intelligence findings from small groups automatically generalize to corporations.** Woolley et al. support group-level intelligence in small groups, not high-technology corporate systems.
- **Do not present category theory/process algebra as already applied successfully to this organizational model.** The search supports mathematical plausibility and future formal direction, not completed validation.
- **Do not reduce constraint to law or ethics alone.** Institutional logics and institutional complexity suggest constraint may include professional, market, state, community, reputational, and identity logics.
- **Do not reduce feasibility to engineering facts alone.** Socio-technical systems and organizational learning imply feasibility includes work design, information flow, operational capacity, memory, and coordination.
- **Do not reduce appetite to pathology.** Appetite/growth pressure can drive exploration, adaptation, and innovation; the model should diagnose dysregulated appetite, not appetite itself.
- **Do not claim VSM is obsolete or insufficient without nuance.** Cybernetics is an important predecessor; critiques such as Achterbergh & Vriens should be used to position complementarity, not dismissal.
- **Do not make the first paper too formal.** The best-supported genre is conceptual/theory-building with a light formal sketch and a future empirical/formal research program.
