# Literature Source Pack: Appetite, Feasibility, and Constraint

This pack condenses the updated `01-literature-raw.md` into sources that are verified and useful for drafting the first paper. It excludes sources that are only weakly connected, unverifiable, or likely to distract reviewers. Claims below are phrased only as strongly as the cited source allows.

## 1. Foundational Sources

These are the 12 sources the paper should almost certainly cite unless the argument is narrowed.

1. Bion, W. R. (1961). *Experiences in Groups and Other Papers*. London: Tavistock Publications. Stable record: https://openlibrary.org/books/OL5821596M/Experiences_in_groups_and_other_papers.
   - Use for: Work-group and basic-assumption group concepts as the primary psychodynamic ancestor.
   - Do not overclaim: Bion does not show that corporations have psyches; he supports a group-dynamics lineage and metaphor.
   - Full-text verification required before final citation.

2. Fraher, A. L. (2004). "Systems Psychodynamics: The Formative Years of an Interdisciplinary Field at the Tavistock Institute." *History of Psychology*, 7(1), 65-84. https://doi.org/10.1037/1093-4510.7.1.65.
   - Use for: Establishing systems psychodynamics as an existing interdisciplinary tradition grounded in psychoanalysis, group relations, and open-systems thinking.
   - Do not overclaim: This is historical grounding, not evidence for the appetite/feasibility/constraint model.

3. French, R. B., & Simpson, P. (2010). "The 'work group': Redressing the balance in Bion's *Experiences in Groups*." *Human Relations*, 63(12), 1859-1878. https://doi.org/10.1177/0018726710365091.
   - Use for: The central corrective that Bion's work-group concept should not be overshadowed by basic-assumption pathology.
   - Do not overclaim: It supports functional mediation between work-group and basic-assumption modes; it does not provide the full appetite/feasibility/constraint model.

4. Morgan, G. (2006). *Images of Organization*. Updated ed. Thousand Oaks, CA: SAGE. ISBN: 9781412939799. Publisher page: https://collegepublishing.sagepub.com/products/images-of-organization-1-229704.
   - Use for: Methodological discipline around metaphor, including the way organization metaphors illuminate and distort.
   - Do not overclaim: Morgan supports careful metaphor use; he does not validate the Freudian analogy.
   - Full-text verification required before final citation.

5. Cyert, R. M., & March, J. G. (1992). *A Behavioral Theory of the Firm* (2nd ed.). Wiley-Blackwell. Original work published 1963. Stable record: https://www.wiley-vch.de/en/areas-interest/finance-economics-law/behavioral-theory-of-the-firm-978-0-631-17451-6.
   - Use for: The main Carnegie-school anchor: firms as boundedly rational, coalitional, procedurally organized systems.
   - Do not overclaim: Our framework must be positioned as an extension or diagnostic redescription, not the first theory to look inside the firm.
   - Full-text verification required before final citation.

6. Smith, W. K., & Lewis, M. W. (2011). "Toward a Theory of Paradox: A Dynamic Equilibrium Model of Organizing." *Academy of Management Review*, 36(2), 381-403. https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.2009.0223.
   - Use for: The primary organization-theory anchor for persistent contradictory demands.
   - Do not overclaim: Organization theory already has mature language for paradox; the paper should not claim to discover contradiction.

7. March, J. G. (1991). "Exploration and Exploitation in Organizational Learning." *Organization Science*, 2(1), 71-87. https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2.1.71.
   - Use for: A canonical organizational-learning tension involving present exploitation and future exploration.
   - Do not overclaim: Exploration/exploitation is not the same as the full triad.

8. Huber, G. P. (1991). "Organizational Learning: The Contributing Processes and the Literatures." *Organization Science*, 2(1), 88-115. https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2.1.88.
   - Use for: Knowledge acquisition, information distribution, information interpretation, and organizational memory.
   - Do not overclaim: Huber supports learning-process vocabulary, not psychodynamic conflict transformation.

9. Weick, K. E., Sutcliffe, K. M., & Obstfeld, D. (2005). "Organizing and the Process of Sensemaking." *Organization Science*, 16(4), 409-421. https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.1050.0133.
   - Use for: The idea that ambiguous circumstances become comprehended situations that can guide action.
   - Do not overclaim: Sensemaking explains interpretation and enactment; it does not endorse our three-force model.

10. Suchman, M. C. (1995). "Managing Legitimacy: Strategic and Institutional Approaches." *Academy of Management Review*, 20(3), 571-610. https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.1995.9508080331.
   - Use for: Constraint as legitimacy: pragmatic, moral, and cognitive approval, plus strategies for gaining, maintaining, and repairing legitimacy.
   - Do not overclaim: Legitimacy is one major constraint dimension, not the whole of constraint.

11. Crawford, V. P., & Sobel, J. (1982). "Strategic Information Transmission." *Econometrica*, 50(6), 1431-1451. https://doi.org/10.2307/1913390.
   - Use for: Formal support that communication under partially conflicting preferences can transmit limited information.
   - Do not overclaim: Cheap-talk models abstract away unconscious defense, identity, law, ethics, and institutional legitimacy.

12. Beer, S. (1985). *Diagnosing the System for Organizations*. Chichester: Wiley. ISBN: 0471906751. Stable record: https://openlibrary.org/books/OL2862901M/Diagnosing_the_system_for_organizations.
   - Use for: Diagnostic organizational cybernetics and the viable-system predecessor to our framework.
   - Do not overclaim: The paper should complement, not dismiss, VSM.
   - Full-text verification required before final citation.

## 2. Supporting Sources

### Synthesis: What Each Literature Gives and Does Not Give

The literature review should not become an encyclopedia. Its organizing logic should be comparative: each literature contributes a necessary piece, but none is sufficient by itself.

| Literature | What it gives us | What it does not give us |
|---|---|---|
| Systems psychodynamics | Anxiety, defense, group dynamics | Formal action sequencing |
| Behavioral theory of the firm | Bounded rationality, coalitions, routines | Psychodynamic diagnostic triad |
| Paradox theory | Contradictory demands | Operator/action-composition model |
| Organizational learning | Acquisition, interpretation, memory | Conflict as computational mediation |
| Game theory | Strategic communication | Legitimacy, identity, unconscious defense |
| Cybernetics | Diagnostic organizational regulation | Appetite/feasibility/constraint vocabulary |
| Formal math | Composition, irreversibility, non-commutativity | Organizational empirical grounding |

### Model Integration Notes

The model should include a power parameter:

`p = relative power distribution among appetite, feasibility, and constraint actors`.

This lets the paper state that the triad is not a balanced committee by default. Which force dominates depends partly on authority, incentives, resource control, dependence, and escalation rights.

The model should also distinguish protocols from adjacent concepts:

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Routine | Recurring pattern of organizational action |
| Capability | Reliable capacity to perform or adapt |
| Protocol | Rule, process, or communicative structure for transforming conflict into action |
| Mediation protocol | A protocol specifically governing how appetite, feasibility, and constraint are surfaced, sequenced, weighted, and resolved |

### Paradox, Ambidexterity, and Institutional Complexity

## 3. Sources to Mention Cautiously

- Kets de Vries (2006), *The Leader on the Couch*.
  - Cautious use: Leadership psychodynamics only.
  - Risk: Pulls the paper toward executive psychology rather than organizational-system intelligence.

- Woolley et al. (2010), "Evidence for a Collective Intelligence Factor."
  - Cautious use: Small-group collective intelligence evidence.
  - Risk: Does not establish corporate-scale intelligence.

- Malone, Laubacher, & Dellarocas (2010), "The Collective Intelligence Genome."
  - Cautious use: Design-pattern vocabulary.
  - Risk: Management magazine article; useful but weaker than peer-reviewed sources.

- Gibson & Birkinshaw (2004), Raisch & Birkinshaw (2008), and Teece et al. (1997).
  - Cautious use: Ambidexterity and capabilities.
  - Risk: The paper must explain why the proposed triad is not just renamed ambidexterity or dynamic capabilities.

- Thornton, Ocasio, & Lounsbury (2012).
  - Cautious use: Institutional logics.
  - Risk: It complicates the triad because appetite, feasibility, and constraint may each be shaped by institutional logics.

- Hickson et al. (1971), Pfeffer (1981), and Pfeffer & Salancik (1978).
  - Cautious use: Power and resource dependence.
  - Risk: These sources may shift the paper from conflict-processing protocols to organizational politics unless used tightly.

- Donaldson & Preston (1995) and Freeman (2010).
  - Cautious use: Stakeholder theory as constraint and value-plurality background.
  - Risk: Normative stakeholder claims must be clearly separated from descriptive organizational-intelligence claims.

- Power (2007).
  - Cautious use: Risk management as governance.
  - Risk: This source is useful for constraint, but can over-specialize the first paper around risk governance if overused.

- Ghani et al. (2018).
  - Cautious use: Formal future-work scaffolding.
  - Risk: Even Ghani et al. can distract reviewers if the paper does not define formal objects.

## 5. Sources to Avoid

- Do not cite Wikipedia, blogs, LinkedIn, Reddit, general web explainers, or uncited encyclopedia pages as academic support.
  - Reason: They are not suitable primary support for a conceptual academic paper.

- Do not cite ResearchGate pages as evidence when the article has a DOI, publisher page, PubMed record, university repository record, or stable library/catalog record.
  - Reason: ResearchGate can help orientation, but it is not the strongest bibliographic authority.

- Do not cite SCIRP "References" pages as sources for the underlying classics.
  - Reason: These pages are not the original source, publisher, or stable scholarly record.

- Do not cite vendor bookstore pages unless no publisher, DOI, library, or university record is available.
